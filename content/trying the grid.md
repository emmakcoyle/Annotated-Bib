**Take me to the River: Immersive Acts in Theater, Ecology, and River Pageantry

Emma Coyle

  

What does it mean to perform with a river rather than in front of it? Contemporary river pageantry, exemplified by the River Clyde Pageant, reaches back through centuries of outdoor [pageantry] traditions, but its modern iteration raises questions about the unique water-based location of these performances. What does the [river] do? What happens to the line between audience and performer when we consider the performances of more-than-human kin? What kind of attention becomes possible when the [pageant]'s performance and the [ecosystem] around it are not considered separate spheres? This talk moves through the conditions of the pageant's own production to ask what kind of [immersion] is being produced when the theatrical and the ecological are happening simultaneously. 

  

Take me to the River is a 10-minute video that uses its form to juxtapose images of the Clyde River and its inhabitants, with images of the [River Clyde Pageant] from its ten years of performances. Within the spoken text and in close attention to the material realities of the [River Clyde Pageant], I am engaging with [Bruno Latour]'s Actor-Network Theory, [Astrida Neimanis’s] [hydrocommons], and [Donna Haraway]’s notions of kinship with the more-than-human.**
