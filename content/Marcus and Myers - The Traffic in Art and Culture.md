**Annotation:**

In *The Traffic in Art and Culture*, Marcus and Myers delve into the complexities of the "art world," a Western-based construct that encompasses a set of rules, methods, and institutional structures governing art and its circulation. The text positions the art world within the broader scope of globalization and identifies it as an ever-expanding entity with far-reaching influence, not just within the domain of visual arts but also across social and cultural fields. Through their ethnographic lens, the authors challenge the presumed objectivity of both anthropology and the art world, noting the extent to which both disciplines have historically positioned themselves as "outsiders." As they study American dynasties and Aboriginal cultures, Marcus and Myers repeatedly encounter curators, museums, and other art-world figures. This intersection led them to realize that the art world itself had not been adequately studied as a culture, despite its pervasive influence.

The authors propose a model for understanding the art world through three central themes: appropriation, boundary, and circulation. These concepts allow them to explore the fluid, dynamic interactions between art and anthropology, suggesting that both fields have played a role in shaping and defining the boundaries of cultural knowledge and artistic production. Marcus and Myers emphasize that the art world's process of "borrowing and stealing" from other academic fields complicates efforts to study it ethnographically, since its practices continuously evolve through cross-disciplinary contamination.

Their work ultimately challenges the traditional separation between the art world and academic disciplines, proposing a more interconnected, ethnographically engaged approach. They argue that art's role in creating and performing cultural difference is akin to anthropology's mission, and both disciplines have often defined themselves in opposition to the cultural contexts they aim to understand. The volume calls for a more nuanced exploration of the entanglements between art, culture, and anthropology, acknowledging the absence of a comprehensive study of the art world outside of those immersed in it.

**Quotation(s):**

"More recently, in contemporary cultural life, art has come to occupy a space long associated with anthropology, becoming one of the main sites for tracking, representing, and performing the effects of difference in contemporary life." (Marcus and Myers, The Traffic in Art and Culture, p. 1)

"This is a world still defined, even with its postmodern attempts at transformation, by the creation of aesthetic experience through the disinterested contemplation of objects as art objects removed from instrumental associations." (Marcus and Myers, The Traffic in Art and Culture, p. 3)
