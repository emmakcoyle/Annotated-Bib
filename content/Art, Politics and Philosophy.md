### Phillips, John. Art, Politics, and Philosophy.

*Reformatted with the help of AI*

**Annotation:**

John Phillips' Art, Politics, and Philosophy is a challenging theoretical exploration that grapples with the dynamics between art, philosophy, and politics. The text is notably dense, particularly for readers unfamiliar with the concepts of structuralism and post-structuralism, and requires an engagement with foundational literary theory to fully appreciate its arguments. Phillips' approach is less about reaching a conclusive or agreeable standpoint and more about exploring pre-existing dynamics within artistic and intellectual practice.

One of the central concepts in Phillips' work is the idea of "null definition" — the notion that something's definition is only possible through its negation or absence. This connects to the broader idea of ineffability in art, where the meaning or existence of an object is not determined by what is present but by what is absent. This theory of art that operates in a space between presence and absence aligns with the ideas explored in Malevich's work, particularly in his "Black Square" painting, which challenges the very notion of representation. For Malevich, art's relationship to the divine and the ineffable is mirrored in the creation of art that denies traditional representational forms. Phillips' exploration of the relationship between mathematics and poetry also ties into these notions, where the abstract or mathematical approach to art creation contrasts with poetry's evasiveness of direct definition. In a similar vein, poetry "circles" its own definition without ever fully defining itself, embodying the paradox of artistic expression.

Phillips defines "Auestics" as a way of reflecting on art that is intricately tied to thinking about the operations of thought itself. This suggests a deeper meta-reflection on art, where the very process of contemplating art becomes part of the artwork's experience. This is also an extension of the concept of an "aesthetic regime" that defines art not merely as a collection of objects but as a system of reflection that enables a broader understanding of aesthetics as a way of thinking.

Ultimately, Phillips' work opens up a discussion about art's capacity to reflect both the visible and the invisible, where its essence is defined by what it does not show, reinforcing the tension between representation and abstraction in the visual arts. For readers familiar with Oulipo's literary constraints, Phillips' arguments about the role of absence in defining presence resonate strongly with these concepts.

**Quotation(s):**

"Auestics then refers to a certain way of reflecting on art that is connected with a way of thinking about the operations of thought." (Phillips, Art, Politics, and Philosophy, p. 151)

"It is the aesthetic regime itself that enables a reflection on aesthetics as an aesthetic regime." (Phillips, Art, Politics, and Philosophy, p. 151)
